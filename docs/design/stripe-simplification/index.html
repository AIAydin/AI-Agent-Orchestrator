Three Stripe-inspired directions

Draft previews for simplifying the Forgeboard UI toward Stripe's design language: one accent color, hairline borders, quiet surfaces, and typography doing the heavy lifting. Each preview covers the two main screens — Home (today's Welcome) and the Board (today's Workspace). Open them, compare, and we'll refine the direction you like.

A — Sidebar dashboard

Closest to the Stripe Dashboard. Persistent left nav with projects, KPI cards up top, recent projects as a clean table, and the board framed by a slim inspector. Familiar, scannable, low-risk.

Open preview →

B — Minimal light

The aggressive simplification. No sidebar, no persistent panels: a centered column at home, and a borderless canvas with one floating toolbar on the board. Almost everything is whitespace and type; chrome appears on demand.

Open preview →

C — Command-first console

Data-table first, like Stripe's payments view. A command bar anchors the top, agent runs become the primary list, and the board is one tab over. Dense but disciplined — for keyboard-heavy daily use.

What "simpler" means here

  • One accent color (#635BFF) reserved for primary actions and links; status comes from small dots, not colored panels.
  • Hairline borders (1px #E3E8EE) and near-invisible shadows instead of heavy frames and layered panels.
  • Eyebrow labels (11px, uppercase, letter-spaced) replace boxed section headers.
  • Secondary actions hide until hover; one obvious primary action per screen.
  • Density comes from tables and lists, not from squeezing cards.
